### Detailed Analysis

#### Task Achievement & Cohesion (TA & CC): Band 7.5 - 8.0

**Strengths 👍:**

- **Perfect Overview:** Your overview is flawless. You correctly identified the two rising trends (in person, telephone) and the one falling trend (letter/email). This is exactly what is required.
- **Logical Structure:** Your chronological split (describing trends before and after April) is the ideal structure for this chart. Your essay is perfectly organized.
- **Excellent Comparisons:** Your mathematical comparisons are a major strength. Identifying that in-person enquiries grew "more than four times" and that letter/email enquiries fell to "almost only a half" of their initial number are high-level analytical points.

**Area for Improvement (Data Precision) ✍️:**
There was one small calculation error. You stated that telephone enquiries "almost doubled," but the number rose from 900 to 1600. While a large increase, it is not almost double. This is a minor slip in an otherwise excellent analysis.

#### Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA): Band 7.5

**Strengths 👍:** Your sentence control is excellent. You used a variety of structures to describe the data clearly and concisely, and there are no sentence fragments. Your grammatical accuracy is very high.

#### Lexical Resource (Vocabulary): Band 6.5 (The Final Area for Polish)

Your vocabulary is good, but a few recurring errors are preventing a higher score.

**Areas for Improvement ✍️:**

- **Amount vs. Number:** You used amount of enquiries. Since "enquiries" are countable, the correct word is number. Remember: amount for things you can't count (e.g., water, time) and number for things you can (e.g., enquiries, people, cars).
- **Inclined:** This word means "tilted" or "leaning." For trends on a graph, the correct verb is increased or rose.
- **It's vs. Its:** This is a very common but critical error.
  - It's always means it is (e.g., "It's a sunny day").
  - Its is the possessive form (e.g., "The company increased its sales").
  - Your sentences reached it's peak and i's initial number should both use its.
