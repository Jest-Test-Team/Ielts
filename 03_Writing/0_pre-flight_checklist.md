# 🚀 IELTS Writing: Pre-Flight Checklist (Read Before Every Timed Practice)

_Use this checklist before every timed writing practice to prevent common errors and maintain consistency._

---

## ☐ **Task 1: The Data Sniper's Code**

### **Pre-Writing Analysis (2 minutes)**

- **1. What is the Y-Axis?**

  - Is it a number, percentage, rate of change, or currency?
  - Say it out loud: "The Y-axis shows [specific unit]"
  - _This prevents the "Population Growth" error where you misidentify data units_

- **2. Who are the subjects?**

  - Point to each line/bar/pie slice and name it clearly
  - Identify all categories, countries, or groups shown
  - _This prevents the "Philippines/Indonesia" error where you confuse similar data points_

- **3. What are the keywords?**
  - Read the prompt's nouns carefully
  - Are they spelled correctly in your mind? (e.g., C-O-P-P-E-R)
  - Check for proper nouns, technical terms, and units
  - _This prevents the "Cooper" error where you misspell key terms_

### **Structure Verification**

- **4. Overview Paragraph:**

  - Will I write exactly ONE overview paragraph?
  - Does it come after the introduction?
  - _This prevents structural confusion_

- **5. Data Selection:**
  - Have I identified the most significant trends/patterns?
  - Am I focusing on the most important data points?
  - _This ensures efficient use of 150 words_

---

## ☐ **Task 2: The Strategist's Code**

### **Question Analysis (1 minute)**

- **1. What is the question type?**
  - Opinion (Agree/Disagree)?
  - Discussion (Both views)?
  - Problem/Solution?
  - Advantages/Disadvantages?
  - _Circle the type before starting_

### **Structure Discipline**

- **2. Four Paragraphs. No More, No Less.**
  - Introduction (2-3 sentences)
  - Body Paragraph 1 (4-5 sentences)
  - Body Paragraph 2 (4-5 sentences)
  - Conclusion (2-3 sentences)
  - _I will NOT write a separate "Overall" paragraph_
  - _This prevents the "Museums" essay structural flaw_

### **Sentence Control (Critical)**

- **3. The 25-Word Iron Law.**

  - I will break complex ideas into shorter, controlled sentences
  - Maximum 25 words per sentence
  - I will NOT write "monster sentences"
  - _This is your most important discipline_

- **4. Hunt the Fragment.**
  - I will NOT start any sentence with `Which` or `By which`
  - I will check every sentence for completeness
  - _This targets your most persistent grammatical error_

### **Content Strategy**

- **5. Clear Position:**

  - Do I have a clear, consistent position throughout?
  - Is my stance obvious from the introduction?
  - _This prevents waffling and confusion_

- **6. Supporting Evidence:**
  - Do I have specific examples for each main point?
  - Are my examples relevant and well-developed?
  - _This ensures strong task achievement_

---

## ☐ **Universal Writing Rules**

### **Time Management**

- **Task 1:** 20 minutes maximum
- **Task 2:** 40 minutes maximum
- **Review:** 5 minutes for both tasks

### **Word Count Discipline**

- **Task 1:** 150+ words (aim for 160-180)
- **Task 2:** 250+ words (aim for 260-280)
- _Count words as you write_

### **Language Control**

- **7. Vocabulary Level:**

  - Am I using appropriate academic vocabulary?
  - Am I avoiding overly complex words I'm unsure about?
  - _This prevents vocabulary errors_

- **8. Grammar Check:**
  - Subject-verb agreement correct?
  - Articles (a/an/the) used properly?
  - Prepositions accurate?
  - _Quick mental scan before finishing_

- [ ] I have read and understood the task requirements
- [ ] I have identified the question type correctly
- [ ] I have planned my structure (4 paragraphs for Task 2)
- [ ] I am ready to write controlled, clear sentences
- [ ] I will avoid fragments and monster sentences
- [ ] I will maintain consistent position throughout
